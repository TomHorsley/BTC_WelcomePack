How Old Is My Dog?

A note on passport date of birth (estimated). This applies predominantly to our rescue work with the municipality shelters rather than the other areas we liberate animals from.

On occasion the date of birth in the passport may not reflect the approximate age we have given for your dog on appeals and adverts. That doesn't mean they aren't the age we stated (estimated), rather that their years on earth has been reassessed AFTER the date of birth was entered on the government system. This would have been done quickly when they first arrived at the shelter, maybe when they were too scared, hurt and neglected to assess fully and accurately, etc. Teeth may look whiter due to lack of good food such as raw bones which actually creates tartar. It could be that their teeth are ground down a little from eating off concrete.

Once an age is determined later by a veterinarian as more accurate, we do not have the option of changing it on the goverment system, and it is that system that dictates all the import paperwork including the passport (which must be consistent with one another) from there-on-in.

Ordinarily any vet / organisation etc. can reassess a dog's age and we would never know they had. Ten different vets would potentially age the same dog differently regardless of where they were in the world. The difference being that their systemes would allow the initial estimate to be updated once a full check-up has taken place.

Along with this, we have cultural mindsets on age, which may view any dog over 5 as being older, regardless of breed or size, with nothing in between. Even specialists in top veterinary hospitals might raise their hands in the air when asked the age of a dog because it's considered a complex area that is based upon more than just our experiences.

Usually we aim to judge within a 1-2yr age period. On rare occasions the passport may say 5-6yrs but when the dog recovers, and looking or moving better for example, they are perhaps only thought to be 3yrs. We will always tell you if the age anomaly is drastic prior to the dog being formally adopted. Whatever the apparent discrepancy between the passport and our adverts, when it does occur you can go by what we told you first and foremost. The passport will not impact insurance etc. as every insurance policy is different whether a senior dog or a younger dog, and will offer what you need if you shop around.
